## Problem

Reproduced 2026-10-17 by the epic #47 correctness review on main (6f3ef40), range 77e04d9..6f3ef40.

1. Over-redaction (new in #42): `redact_text('GET http://host:8080/api?email=bob@example.com 200')` → `GET http://host:<redacted>@example.com 200`; `redact_text('https://host:443/users/@alice')` → `https://host:<redacted>@alice`. 77e04d9 left both unchanged. Cause: `src/bricksmith/ops/redact.py:39` and `:148-156` read `host:port` as `user:` and take any later `@` in the path or query as the end of the credential.
2. Partial leak (pre-existing, also on 77e04d9): `redact_text('https://user:p@ss/w@host/x')` → `https://user:<redacted>@ss/w@host/x`. Cause: `redact.py:154` searches for `/` after the first `@`, so `rfind` stops at the first `@`, although the docstring says the password may hold `/` or `@`.

Consequence: logs and reports lose the port, path and query of ordinary credential-free URLs, and part of a password with `@` then `/` is printed. Keep #41's linear-time bound.

## Proposal

- A URL whose `host:port` is not followed by an `@` separator keeps its port, path and query: `redact_text('GET http://host:8080/api?email=bob@example.com 200')` and `redact_text('https://host:443/users/@alice')` come back unchanged.
- A password holding `@` and then `/` is masked whole: `https://user:p@ss/w@host/x` becomes `https://user:<redacted>@host/x`.
- Every URL-credential case already covered (`pa/ss@host`, `p@ss@host`, `user:pw@host`) keeps its output, and #41's linear-time bound, #56's plural/suffix masking and #55's block-scalar masking are unchanged.

## Out of Scope

- Percent-decoding or fully parsing URLs: the pass stays a regex plus a bounded scan.
- Telling a password that contains `/` followed by `@` from a path with an `@` handle when the password looks like a port (all digits, at most 5): treated as a port. ACCEPTED, see Risks.
- Changing keyword masking (`_mask_assignments`) or any non-URL pattern.

## Design

- **Port is not a credential.** When the text after `user:` is 1 to 5 digits followed by `/`, `?`, `#` or the end of the token, the match is `host:port` and the URL is returned unchanged. Digits followed by `@` stay a (numeric) password.
- **The separator is the last `@` of the token that is not directly preceded by `/`, searched before the first `?` or `#`.** A `/` inside the password is therefore tolerated, an `@` handle after a path slash is not a separator, and a query holding an email is left out of the credential. When no `@` qualifies before `?`/`#`, the search falls back to the whole token, so a password holding `?` is still masked (leak-safe over exact).
- **Linear time.** The scan uses `str.find`/`rfind` that only move left from the end bound, and the regex is unchanged apart from nothing new, so #41's bound holds; a bounded-time test covers a long run of `@` characters.
- Binding: the redaction contract of #41, #56 and #55 (same module and test file); no grant, hook, CI or executable-surface change.

## Alternatives Considered

- Validating the host after each candidate `@` with a hostname regex: every candidate in `p@ss/w@host/x` and in `pw@host/api?e=a@b.com` looks like a host, so it cannot choose between them.
- Masking only up to the first `/` after the first `@` (status quo): leaks the rest of passwords holding `@` then `/`.

## Open Decisions

- none — the ambiguous cases are decided in Design and accepted in Risks.

## Risks

- ACCEPTED — a password made only of 1 to 5 digits and containing `/` before its `@` (`user:123/abc@host`) is read as a port and not masked; the epic policy prefers keeping ordinary `host:port/path` URLs readable, and such a password is not realistic.
- ACCEPTED — a password ending in `/` right before its separator `@` keeps the `@` preceded-by-slash exclusion and may be partly shown; vanishingly rare.
- A regression of the linear-time bound: mitigated by the bounded-time test.
