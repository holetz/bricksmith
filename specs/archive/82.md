## Problem

Discrimination and a global calibration ratio do not catch the failures model governance looks for in rating models: a grade whose observed default rate leaves its predicted band, grades out of order, discrimination decaying in early horizons before the full target matures, or a changed exclusion mix in the scored population. A grade with few expected events must not hide behind a low count.

## Proposal

- Per-grade calibration: observed/expected with traffic-light bands, Jeffreys test (Beta(D+0.5, N-D+0.5) one-sided p-value) and exact binomial test, with an optional correlation-adjusted binomial variant; skipped below a minimum of expected (not observed) events.
- Probability horizon rescaling (`1-(1-p)^(k/h)`) so early horizons compare with the right expectation.
- Grade ordering: count of inversions between consecutive grades, with limits.
- Early-warning discrimination: KS/Gini at declared shorter horizons (e.g. 3, 6, 9 periods), each with its own maturation lag and metric identity.
- Exclusion composition: PSI of the first-exclusion-reason mix on scored populations, using the stage cascade (spec STG).
- Bad-rate control chart per segment centred on the development median with sigma bands.
- All results flow through the declared model monitoring tables and the shared limits/persistence.

## Out of Scope

- Performance estimation before labels mature (CBPE-style); expected default rate is shown, labelled as expected.

- `uv run pytest tests/unit/monitor/test_credit_stats.py -q` — the incomplete beta matches `I_x(1,1)=x`, the symmetry `I_x(a,b)=1-I_{1-x}(b,a)` and a numerical integration; the binomial tail equals a direct `math.comb` sum; Jeffreys and binomial p-values fall when observed defaults exceed the prediction; the adjusted p-value tends to the unadjusted direction and is larger than it for a positive correlation when defaults exceed the prediction; rescaling is identity at `k=h`; inversions count strict reversals only.

## Design

- 2026-11-24 — **Opt-in families on the existing tables.** Five families join model monitoring: `grade_calibration` (`grade_oe`, `grade_jeffreys_p`, `grade_binomial_p`, `grade_binomial_adjusted_p`), `grade_order` (`grade_inversions`), `early_warning` (`early_ks`, `early_gini`, `early_oe`), `exclusion_mix` (`exclusion_psi`) and `bad_rate_chart` (`bad_rate`). They run when the declaration carries a `credit` block (and the roles each needs) or when `metrics` lists them; a declaration without `credit` keeps exactly today's families, rows and lights. Every result flows through the shared limits, rules, trend, persistence, alerts and gate, and is written to the existing results and details tables.
- **Per-grade rows.** A per-grade result's `column` is `<grade column>=<grade value>`, so a limit rule can select a grade with the existing `column` selector. The calibration table per grade (count, mean expected probability, observed rate) is written as details of the `grade_inversions` result, in grade order.
- **Tests.** `grade_jeffreys_p = BetaCDF(PD; D+0.5, N-D+0.5)` and `grade_binomial_p = P(X >= D | N, PD) = I_PD(D, N-D+1)` with `PD` the grade's mean (rescaled) probability; both are one-sided against underestimation, so a small p-value is bad (default limits `lower`, green from 0.05, red below 0.01). `grade_binomial_adjusted_p` is the one-factor (Vasicek) large-portfolio tail `1 - Φ((√(1-ρ)Φ⁻¹(D/N) - Φ⁻¹(PD))/√ρ)`, computed only when `correlation` is declared. `grade_oe` is observed/expected with the `calibration_ratio` bands (0.7–1.3 green, 0.5–1.5 yellow).
- **Expected-events volume gate.** A grade whose expected events (sum of its probabilities) are below `min_expected_events` (default 5) is `insufficient_volume` with that reason, whatever its observed count; `min_rows` does not apply to per-grade results. `grade_inversions` compares only grades that pass the gate.
- **Horizons.** `credit.horizon` (h) is the probability's horizon in observed periods; a label covering k periods compares with `1-(1-p)^(k/h)`. The calibration label covers `label_horizon` (default h). Each early-warning horizon names its own label column, its k and its lag (default k observed periods, or `{"months": n}`); its results carry `column` = that label column, so each horizon has its own series, rules and alerts.
- **Exclusion mix.** `exclusion_psi` is the PSI of the `credit.exclusion` column (the stage cascade's `exclusion_first`, null meaning not excluded) between development and the period, null being its own category; details carry the per-reason shares.
- **Bad-rate chart.** `bad_rate` (observed events / rows) per segment is judged by default as a control chart (|z| beyond 2 yellow, beyond 3 red) whose centre is the **median** of the development per-period bad rates and whose spread is their sample standard deviation — development only, unlike the control baselines of decision 0016.
- **Engines.** Per-grade and per-horizon sums (`rows`, observed events, expected events under an exponent) are one `Frame.sums` reduction on rows or Spark (built-in functions only, so Free Edition runs it); the statistics run on the driver over those small rows. Early-warning KS and Gini reuse the shared `measure` core with the horizon's label.

### Assumptions

- `min_expected_events` defaults to 5 — a conventional floor for binomial approximations; it is configuration (Design, Validation).
- The adjusted binomial uses the large-portfolio Vasicek approximation (observed rate D/N as the portfolio rate) rather than a finite-portfolio convolution (Design).
- An early-warning lag defaults to its horizon k in observed periods (Design).

## Risks

- Numeric accuracy of the continued-fraction incomplete beta in extreme tails — covered by identities and a numerical integration in unit tests.
- A `fail_on: red` gate starts failing when a user declares `credit` (new default limits) — opt-in by design and documented.
- The Vasicek approximation is coarse for small grades — documented as such and only computed on a declared correlation.
