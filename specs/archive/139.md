## Problem

Data stability (`bricksmith.monitor.stability`, epic 64) profiles every period of a task's output, judges it
with traffic lights, an OLS trend check and persistence alerts, and gates the task. A critical review against
current data-observability practice found three classes of gaps:

- **Statistical soundness.** Measured on the real `trend_check` with pure noise, window 12 flags 2.9% of periods
  red against a nominal 0.27% (about 11x; 35x at the 6-point minimum); a single past spike masks a later 5σ jump;
  a seasonal December peak is red; a one-row change on a flat series is red with infinite deviation; `row_count`
  can never be trended. Measured with the repository's `psi_terms` under no change, a 30-row segment (the default
  `min_rows`) is red on PSI 84% of the time and a 100-row segment yellow-or-worse 62% of the time.
- **Operations.** No human decision can be recorded on stability periods or alerts: a period cannot be accepted,
  excluded from the trend line and baselines, or declared a new regime; a known critical alert fails the gate
  every run; backfills of periods older than `refresh_periods` stay invisible; earlier judgments are overwritten;
  a configuration change cannot be previewed.
- **Coverage, triage and visibility.** Schema drift, missing, late or unsettled periods, duplicate keys and
  vanished categories are not monitored; one bad load produces one notification per series; no dashboard or
  management surface is shipped, only printed views.
## Proposal

Seventeen member specs in five waves:

1. **Trend statistics** — prediction-interval z with Student-t quantiles and an effect-size floor (S1); trend on
   every directional metric including `row_count` (S2); anomalous periods kept out of the fit and rolling
   baselines with a robust fit (S3); seasonality, business days and sustained-shift detectors (S4);
   sample-size-aware PSI limits (S16).
2. **Operational decisions and reprocessing** — append-only period marks: accepted, excluded, regime change,
   invalid (S5); alert actions, typed resolutions and risk acceptance lifted from model monitoring into the shared
   core (S6); explicit and runner-driven reprocessing of periods (S7); append-only judgment history (S8).
3. **Coverage** — schema drift and cross-version table metrics (S9); expected cadence, late and unsettled periods
   (S10); duplicate-key, distinct-count, vanished-category, quantile and optional JS/Wasserstein metrics (S11).
4. **Triage and alert noise** — incidents with root-cause ranking, digest and re-notification policy and a noise
   budget (S12); lineage-aware downstream suppression, severity score and owner routing (S13); backtest of a
   judgment change over stored profiles (S17).
5. **Visibility and management** — dashboard data contract plus a generated AI/BI dashboard as a bundle resource
   (S14); a local operations console over the Databricks SDK, deployable as a Databricks App, with rules read from
   a Delta table (S15).
## Out of Scope

- Replacing the engine with Databricks data quality monitoring / Lakehouse Monitoring: the framework keeps an
  engine-independent core that runs in memory, on Free Edition and in any Spark; native monitors may be compared
  in docs.
- External forecasting dependencies (Prophet, statsmodels) in the pure core.
- Model monitoring features beyond what the shared core changes (trend statistics and the lifted actions apply
  to both monitors).

## Validation

- Wave 1: seeded simulations show false-alarm rates within tolerance of nominal; the masking, seasonal and flat
  series fixtures behave as specified; `row_count` trends without limits.
- Wave 2: marks and actions change gates, trends and baselines deterministically and are reversible; a backfill of
  an old period is re-judged; history keeps earlier judgments.
- Wave 3: schema, cadence and new metrics fixtures alert in pure and Spark engines with identical rows.
- Wave 4: one injected break yields one incident, one digest and silent labeled downstream incidents.
- Wave 5: the Free Edition proof deploys the dashboard and drives a mark and a reprocess from the console.

## Alternatives Considered

- **Adopt Databricks data quality monitoring (anomaly detection and data profiling) instead of the in-house
  engine.** Rejected as a replacement: anomaly detection only models freshness and completeness (row-count lower
  bound) and exposes no sensitivity; data profiling recomputes metrics per refresh without first-publication
  revisions, period marks or a task gate; neither runs in memory or on every engine, which the free-first test rule
  needs. Kept as a reference for the output model: `system.data_quality_monitoring.table_results` stores
  last, min and max predicted values (the band), which S1 and S14 adopt.
  docs.databricks.com/aws/en/data-governance/unity-catalog/data-quality-monitoring/anomaly-detection/
- **Rolling z-score over a training window (Elementary, dbt-expectations).** Simpler than OLS but blind to
  trends; S1 keeps the line and fixes its scale; dbt-expectations' log differences inspire `trend.transform: log`.
  docs.elementary-data.com/data-tests/how-anomaly-detection-works · github.com/metaplane/dbt-expectations
- **Prophet / STL / S-H-ESD forecasting.** Better seasonality but adds heavy dependencies and needs several cycles;
  monthly tables rarely have them (Bigeye requires three cycles). S4 implements seasonal-naive and calendar
  normalization in the pure core instead. docs.bigeye.com/docs/autothresholds
- **SQL alerts (v2) as the gate.** A native alternative to the runner gate; offered optionally in S14, the run
  gate stays the contract because it blocks dependents inside the same plan.
- **Fixed PSI thresholds (status quo).** Rejected for small samples: under no change PSI ≈ (1/n + 1/m)·χ²(B−1)
  (Yurdakul & Naranjo, files.wmich.edu/s3fs-public/attachments/u730/2022/PSIfinal.pdf); S16.
- **Freezing past judgments versus recomputing them.** Soda freezes, Databricks profiling recomputes; S8 keeps the
  current recompute-on-change behavior for the current tables and adds an append-only history so both views exist.
- Human feedback patterns referenced: Monte Carlo (anomalies excluded from training, "mark as normal", exclusion
  windows), Bigeye (adapt / do not adapt), Metaplane ("include observations since"), Elementary
  (`anomaly_exclude_metrics`), Databricks ("not an issue" for 7 days). They inform S5 and S6.

## Risks

- Statistical changes alter judgment fingerprints and re-judge windows once; each member documents a migration
  note.
- Wave 5 (dashboard JSON, console) adds a maintained surface; it stays a thin client over public CLI/API
  contracts, as the delivery-surface rules require.
