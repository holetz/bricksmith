## Problem

There is no way to record a human decision about a period: a known event (a migration, a campaign, a
one-off load) cannot be marked as accepted, cannot be kept out of the trend line and baselines, and a legitimate
permanent change cannot be declared as a new normal. The only levers are editing limits, rules or the baseline in
code, which re-judges the whole window for everyone (`stability/engine.py:140-148`). Epic 64 deferred the human
workflow, and spec 71 delivered it for model monitoring only, explicitly leaving stability out.

## Proposal

- New append-only table `stability_marks` (dataset, period, optional segment/column/metric selectors, `mark`,
  reason, author, created_at, valid scope), with the latest mark per selector winning (same ordering rules as
  `monitor/model/actions.py:59-126`, lifted into the shared core).
- Marks: `accepted` (the period's results keep their value and color but are labeled accepted and do not count
  for the gate or open alerts), `excluded` (never in a trend history or a `previous`/`rolling` baseline, through
  the excluded-periods input), `regime_change` (the trend history and rolling baseline restart at this period),
  `invalid` (the period is being reprocessed: judged `informational`, kept out of everything until unmarked).
- Marks enter the judgment fingerprint (a marks digest), so a new mark re-judges the window deterministically
  on the next run; the run reads marks of the window only.
- CLI: `bricksmith stability mark|unmark|marks` emitting the row and an `INSERT`, like the model action verbs;
  stored results carry `mark` and `mark_reason`.

## Out of Scope

- Alert-level actions (separate spec).
- A UI (the local console spec builds on these verbs).
- Approval chains.

## Validation

- An `excluded` period is absent from the trend history and rolling baselines of later periods; removing the
  mark restores the original judgment.
- `regime_change` restarts the trend: the period after a level shift is green after `min_points` periods.
- `accepted` red results do not fail the gate and stay visible with the mark.

## Design

- Marks are an append-only `marks` table (`mark_id`, `dataset`, `period`, `segment`, `column`, `metric`, `mark`,
  `reason`, `author`, `valid_until`, `created_at`); the latest row per selector `(period, segment, column, metric)`
  wins, ordered by UTC `created_at` then `mark_id` (the ordering key of `monitor/actions.py`). `unmarked` is the row
  `unmark` appends; it cancels the selector. A mark with `valid_until` stops holding after that date (inclusive).
- `excluded`, `regime_change` and `invalid` are period-wide (no segment/column/metric selector); `accepted` may carry
  selectors. A result takes the most specific matching mark.
- `excluded` and `invalid` periods feed `compute(excluded=...)`; `invalid` results are judged `informational`.
  `regime_change` clears the trend history at that period and cuts `previous`/`rolling` baselines to periods at or
  after it; the trend seed ignores stored periods before the latest regime and excluded periods.
- `accepted` keeps value and color; labeled results do not count in the gate, and an open alert whose last period is
  accepted for its series is held out of the open count like a risk acceptance.
- Results carry `mark` and `mark_reason` (empty without a mark). The run reads marks from the earliest period it loads
  (window plus baseline and trend history) onward.
- The judgment fingerprint takes the digest of the effective marks, absent when none so existing fingerprints stay
  equal; expiry therefore re-judges the window.
- `marks` joins the stability `KINDS` (the hook declares it with an empty write that creates it).

### Assumptions

- Selectors on `excluded`/`regime_change`/`invalid` are refused: baselines and trend restart are period-wide.
- `mark` and `mark_reason` are added to `results` only, not to `results_history`.

## Alternatives Considered

- Reusing the `actions` table with a marks action: rejected, actions key on alerts and marks on periods.
- Writing marks into stored results only: rejected, a new mark must re-judge deterministically through the fingerprint.

## Open Decisions

- none — every choice is recorded in `## Design`.

## Risks

- An `accepted` mark can hide a real problem indefinitely; require a reason and optional `valid_until`, and
  show active marks on the dashboard.
