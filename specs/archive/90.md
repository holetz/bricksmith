## Problem

Found by the epic 85 correctness review (reproduced). With a `{"months": n}` maturation lag, `shift_months` only clamps the day down (`src/bricksmith/monitor/model/engine.py:403-404`) and the data period must match a loaded period exactly (`engine.py:417`). Closings are the last day of the period, so a monitoring month shorter than its target month loses the target: periods `2025-01-31 … 2025-05-31`, lag 1 month → `2025-03-31 → 2025-02-28` (found), `2025-04-30 → 2025-03-30` (**None**, reported `data_period_unavailable`), `2025-05-31 → 2025-04-30` (found). Every April, June, September, November and February closing is wrongly reported immature. `docs/how-to/model-monitoring.md:167` promises "the day clamps to the month's end"; the unit test at `tests/unit/monitor/model/test_model_engine.py:320` only covers shifts landing on a shorter month.

## Proposal

- When the source period is the last day of its month, shift to the last day of the target month (end-of-month arithmetic); otherwise keep the current day clamp.
- Unit tests for every month length (31→30, 30→31, 31→28/29, 28→31, leap years) and for a lag of several months; parity between the rows and Spark paths if both shift.

## Out of Scope

- Observed lags (`{"periods": n}` or a bare integer): they count loaded periods, not months, and need no month arithmetic.
- Fiscal or business calendars, week or quarter units: lags stay whole calendar months.
- A Spark path for the shift: only the Python engine shifts months, so there is no second implementation to keep in parity.

## Design

- 2026-11-24 — **Month-end rule.** For the day-bearing formats (`YYYY-MM-DD`, `YYYYMMDD`), when the source day is the last day of its own month the shifted result is the last day of the target month; otherwise the existing clamp `min(day, last)` stands. Month-only formats are unchanged. Rationale: closings are the last day of the period, so the lag means "the same closing, n months earlier", which is the month end regardless of month length.
- A source date that is not a real date still returns `None` (validated before the shift).
- `resolve_data_period` is unchanged: it keeps requiring an exact match on a loaded period, so a missing month is never replaced by a neighbour.

### Assumptions

- Mid-month sources keep today's clamp (day 30 shifted into February still yields the 28th/29th); only the last day of the source month is promoted. Depends on: `## Proposal`.

## Alternatives Considered

- Always clamp to the target month's last day when the source day exceeds it, and keep the day otherwise: rejected, it is today's behaviour and is the bug (30 April to 30 March, while the closing is 31 March).
- Match the data period by month ignoring the day: rejected, it would let a mid-month period resolve to a different day's data and weakens the exact-match guarantee of decision 0018.

## Open Decisions

- none — the rule is fully determined by the proposal and the reproduced bug.

## Risks

- A mid-month period whose day equals the last day of a short month (for example 28 February in a non-leap year) is now treated as a closing and maps to the 31st of a long month. ACCEPTED — a non-leap 28 February is indistinguishable from a closing and the closing reading is the intended one.

## Outcome

Calendar-month maturation lags now clamp to the month end. `shift_months` in `src/bricksmith/monitor/model/engine.py` maps a period that is the last day of its month to the last day of the target month, so month-end closings (for example `2025-04-30` with a one-month lag) resolve `2025-03-31` instead of reporting `data_period_unavailable`. Mid-month days keep their day and still clamp down to the target month's length. The model monitoring guide states the rule.

Tests added in `tests/unit/monitor/model/test_model_engine.py`: a 13-case month-length matrix (28/29/30/31 transitions, leap years, 12-month and multi-month lags, the compact `YYYYMMDD` format, mid-month days) and a closing-series test through `resolve_data_period`.

Left out: periods in other formats (quarters and similar) keep returning no value, as before. No release obligation applies: the repo keeps no changelog and no standard attaches one to the merge. The branch was reviewed against `origin/main`, which had not moved. The gate was re-run green (see the report). No flow defects were found, so nothing was captured.
