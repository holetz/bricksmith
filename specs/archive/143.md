## Problem

The trend is a straight line through the last `window` values (`trend.py:1-5`). Monthly and daily tables
are usually seasonal (month-end, December, weekdays), and a straight line flags every seasonal peak: measured
on the real `trend_check`, a monthly series with a 40% December peak reports the expected December as red
(deviation 4.35). Volume also depends on the number of business days in a period, which no metric normalizes.

## Proposal

- `trend.season: <n>` (e.g. 12 for monthly, 7 for daily): fit the line on the seasonally adjusted series
  (seasonal-naive differences or additive seasonal means over at least two cycles), falling back to the plain
  line with an explicit `insufficient_history` reason while fewer than two cycles exist.
- Optional calendar normalization for volume metrics: `trend.per: period | business_day` with a declared
  calendar (weekends plus a list of holidays), so `row_count` per business day is trended.
- Record the model used on each result (`trend_model`) for audit.
- Next to the point check, an optional sustained-shift detector (`trend.drift: cusum | ewma`, CUSUM k = 0.5 / h = 5
  or EWMA λ = 0.2): a 3σ point rule needs about 44 periods on average to signal a 1σ shift, CUSUM/EWMA about 10
  at a similar in-control run length — on monthly data that is years versus months.
- Change-point proposals: when a level shift is detected (PELT-style over the stored series), the result carries
  `change_point_suggested` so a human can confirm it with a `regime_change` mark instead of living with alerts for a
  whole window.
- References: Bigeye needs three cycles to model a seasonality; Elementary `seasonality: day_of_week`; Lawson,
  *Time-weighted control charts*; NIST e-Handbook §6.3.2.3 (CUSUM).
## Out of Scope

- External forecasting libraries (Prophet, statsmodels): the pure core stays dependency-free.
- Holiday calendars for specific countries shipped in the library; projects declare their own.

## Design

- `TrendPolicy` gains `season` (0 = off, else an integer >= 2), `per` (`period` | `business_day`), `calendar`
  (`weekend` weekday numbers, default Saturday and Sunday, plus `holidays` ISO dates), `drift` (`none` | `cusum` |
  `ewma`) and `change_point` (bool). `to_dict` writes a new key only when it is not its default, so every existing
  declaration keeps its judgment fingerprint.
- Seasonal model: additive seasonal means. The line (the declared `fit`) and the seasonal effects (mean, or median
  for a robust `fit`, centred to sum zero) are backfitted for five rounds over the finite history; the expectation is
  `line(k) + effect(phase(k))`. A multiplicative season is `transform: log`. The residual scale uses
  `m - 2 - (season - 1)` degrees of freedom and the prediction error adds `1 / n_phase` for the estimated effect.
- History keeps its phase: with `season`, a period left out of the history (exclusion, a missing period) is stored as
  a NaN placeholder (`remember`), so the position modulo `season` of every value stays true. The window is
  `max(window, 2 * season)` periods.
- Fewer than two observations in any phase (fewer than two cycles), or no positive degrees of freedom, falls back to
  the plain line and says so: `trend_model` is `line:insufficient_history` (versus `seasonal` or `line`).
- `per: business_day` divides `row_count` by the number of business days of its period (day, month periods) before
  the trend; the calendar is the project's, the library ships no holidays. A period with no business day is not
  trended. `trend_model` carries the suffix `+business_day`.
- `drift` runs on the signed standardized trend deviations of every judged period (kept or excluded: exclusion keeps
  a red period out of the line but it must still feed the detector). CUSUM k = 0.5, h = 5 (two-sided for `both`);
  EWMA lambda = 0.2 with a 3-sigma exact variance limit. `drift_status` is `green` or `red`. Stored
  `trend_deviation` seeds the scores of a rewritten window.
- `change_point`: an exact penalized L2 segmentation (optimal partitioning, the PELT objective without pruning:
  series are tens of points) with the BIC penalty `2 ln(n)` over the same scores; `change_point_suggested` is true
  when the latest change point leaves at least two periods after it and moves the level in the worse direction.
- Durable rule: seasonality, calendar and shift detection decide how a value is judged; values stay stored and
  reported as before. Model monitoring gets the seasonal trend through the shared policy but rejects `per`, `drift`
  and `change_point` (stability only).

### Assumptions

- `drift_status` has no persistence alert of its own: it is reported, not alerted (the spec asks for a detector and
  a suggestion, not a new alert kind). Section: Open Decisions.
- Only the additive seasonal means model ships; seasonal-naive differences are not implemented (the spec lists them as
  an alternative inside the parenthesis).

## Alternatives Considered

- Seasonal-naive differences (y_t - y_{t-season}): needs one more cycle for the same effect and doubles the noise;
  additive means use every cycle.
- STL / Prophet / statsmodels: rejected, the pure core stays dependency-free.
- Business-day normalization for every metric: rejected, only counts depend on the number of days; `row_count` only.

## Open Decisions

- Whether `drift_status` should open a `drift` alert kind: decided by the first real use of the columns, a follow-up
  spec if wanted.

## Risks

- Two seasonal cycles of history are needed; new tables stay on the plain line for a long time.

## Outcome

Shipped: seasonality, business-day volume and sustained-shift detection in the stability trend, pure Python with no new dependency.

- `trend.season: <n>` fits a line plus additive seasonal effects (mean, or median for a robust `fit`) on the history; it needs two observations in every phase and otherwise falls back to the plain line with `trend_model: line:insufficient_history`. A history that leaves a period out keeps its phase (`NaN` placeholder), and a judgment made by the fallback line never excludes its period from the history.
- `trend.per: business_day` with a declared `calendar` (weekend weekdays and holidays) trends `row_count` per business day; the library ships no holidays.
- `trend.drift: cusum | ewma` and `trend.change_point` fill `drift_status` and `change_point_suggested` next to the point check; they are reported, never alerted.
- New result columns `trend_model`, `drift_status`, `change_point_suggested` (added by table evolution); defaults serialize to nothing, so existing declarations keep their judgment fingerprint. The model monitor takes `season` and rejects `per`, `drift` and `change_point`.
- Also fixed: the stored-history seed skipped metrics without a limit direction (`row_count`, `mean`, ...), so their history never seeded a rewritten window.

Left out: seasonal-naive differences (only additive means ship); a `drift` alert kind; business-day normalization of metrics other than `row_count`. The drift detectors read deviations from the adaptive line, so they flag a sustained departure the line keeps lagging rather than a step it absorbs in a few periods (that is what `change_point_suggested` covers). A series with a gap needs to be contiguous for the phase to hold.
